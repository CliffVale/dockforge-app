Docking Lab ⚗️

Dock a real ligand into a real target using AutoDock Vina — compiled to your browser as Webina. Nothing is uploaded anywhere; your CPU does the science.

💡 Heads-up: the Webina app lives inside the frame below and loads its docking engine (a few MB) on first use — give it a few seconds. It needs a Chromium-based browser (Chrome/Edge/Brave). Everything runs locally.

Step 1 · Get your example files

Each “example kit” = receptor (the target, ready for docking) + ligand (a molecule that really binds it, taken from the crystal structure) + recommended grid-box numbers from that ligand’s pocket.

Box numbers were computed from the co-crystallized drug’s position (pocket padded by ~8–12 Å) — lesson 6 explains why. Netropsin sits in the minor groove; PDBe’s SITE annotation marks the contact bases (DA6–DT7, DA–T8, DT9–DG10 region), so you can check your result against experiment. For 1BNA (naked B-DNA, no ligand inside) you’ll pick your own box — or run blind docking across the whole helix.

Step 2 · Load files into Webina & dock

  1. In the Webina app below, open the “Input Parameters” tab and click “Use Example Files” if you just want a dry run — or click Choose Files and select the receptor + ligand .pdb files you downloaded above (Webina converts them to PDBQT for you).
  2. Enter the Grid box center and size from the table above (or keep the default box for the dry run).
  3. Set Exhaustiveness = 8 for your first run.
  4. Press “Start Docking” and wait — the DNA example typically takes a couple of minutes on a laptop.
  5. When it finishes, download the output PDBQT (poses) and the results table, then open the Result Viewer and drag the files in to see where the ligand wants to live. 🎉
